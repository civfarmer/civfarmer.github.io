1 · The call

What was known on the Tuesday, and what “done” would mean

The chair rings in March. The finance manager has resigned with a month’s notice; the chief executive, who had been covering much of the finance work herself, retires at the end of the quarter. Neither departure is anybody’s fault.

The management accounts have shown a loss for each of the last four months, in a format the board stopped reading some time ago, and a trustee who looked at the bank statements over the weekend no longer believes the year will end on budget. The five opening questions produce a clock, a decision-maker, a route to the records and, after an hour, a definition of done.

The first days

  • Three bank accounts. The restricted balances reconcile to the bank: the best news of the engagement, and the first thing the funders will ask about. Unrestricted cash covers this payroll and most of the next.
  • The law is a charitable company’s: Companies Act duties on the trustees, the creditor duty in prospect if the forecast confirms what the bank statements suggest, and the serious-incident guidance to be read with the forecast, not before it.
  • The finance manager, still working her notice, is the most useful person in the building. She is asked to stay an extra fortnight at a premium, and does.
  • A second administrator is added to the finance system on day one. A change of bank details on a supplier’s record, made a month earlier, is checked by phone and is genuine.
  • Staff, funders and the commissioner who manages the transport contract are told what is known.

Definition of done

  • Payroll funded through the quarter
  • Restricted funds verified
  • A going-concern assessment the auditor can sign
  • Services maintained or transferred, not abandoned
  • No formal process unless the board chooses one
The order of work

Cash, law, people, reputation. The order the engagement ran in, and the one to copy: the forecast first, then the duties it triggers, then the people, then what is said outside.

Before the operator arrived

Wanting to reassure, the chair told the staff that payroll was “safe through the summer”. The forecast at the end of week one would show five weeks.

2 · Diagnosis

A deficit with a plain cause, and no forecast to see it by

The decisive constraint

No one can say what the cash position will be in six weeks, so every decision is being taken in the dark.

Where the deficit comes from

Once the service lines are separated, the cause is no mystery.

  1. The transport contract was re-let the previous spring at a price that does not cover its overheads.
  2. The largest grant, which had paid quarterly in advance for six years, moved to payment in arrears in the autumn.

The three risks

  1. Payroll in week seven on the downside case.
  2. The transport renewal on the same terms as before.
  3. A finance assistant, told to “keep everything paid”, drawing on restricted money without realising it.
The grant

The same money, later. The largest grant had paid quarterly in advance for six years; from the autumn it pays in arrears, and the first payment on the new terms is the one the forecast turns on.

3 · Stabilise

The thirteen weeks

Dated events in the case. In week five the board resolved to offer the commissioner a transfer of the transport service; the transfer went ahead after a three-month delay, outside this window.

Controls that held

  • The scenario matrix and its triggers are minuted, so the board knows in advance what each number means.
  • The payment schedule replaces the old authorisation limits. The treasurer, who has signed payments for eleven years and regards the schedule as a slight on the staff, is asked to be its second key, and becomes its most reliable one.
  • Once the forecast confirms that the charity’s ability to operate is at risk, the Commission is told, with the plan.
  • Nothing is said to staff or funders beyond what is known.
What held it together

A forecast the board had never had, a second key on every payment, and creditors spoken to before the due date rather than after it.

Embed and leave

  • The going-concern pack is with the auditor before the auditor asks: the forecast, the sensitivities, the principal risks, the post-balance-sheet events log, and the minute setting out the basis, with a material uncertainty disclosed.
  • The one-page pack becomes the board’s standing finance report.
  • The payment schedule stays, at a higher threshold.
  • A reserves policy with a target is adopted, with a three-year plan to reach it.
  • A new finance manager and an interim chief executive are in post by week eleven; the handover document goes to the chair in week thirteen.
The blank line

The options appraisal had a legality column whose pensions line was left blank. The four transport staff were the charity’s only remaining members of the Local Government Pension Scheme; transferring them would have made the charity an exiting employer, and the fund’s actuary put the exit payment at more than a year’s deficit.

The board found out in week seven. The transfer went ahead only after the fund agreed a deferred debt agreement under which the charity keeps paying contributions: a three-month delay, and a liability that will sit in the accounts for years.

5 · What it cost, and what to take from it

The wrong turn, the dilemma, and the shape of the outcome

What went wrong

On the morning of the call, before the operator had arrived, the chair told the staff that payroll was “safe through the summer”. The forecast at the end of week one showed five weeks. Correcting it cost more trust than the bad news would have, and two of the best coordinators left the following month. The rule, say only what is known, was right, and had been broken before anyone thought to apply it.

The dilemma the method did not settle

Whether to keep the befriending scheme at all. It lost money; it was the service the charity was founded to run; and its volunteers were the charity’s best ambassadors. The numbers said close it. The purposes said keep it.

The board kept it in a smaller form, funded from unrestricted reserves for two years with a review at eighteen months, and two trustees recorded their dissent. The method could only make sure the decision was taken knowingly and written down.

Run-rate deficit. Unrestricted cash at week thirteen covers about three months of payments. These figures are invented to show the shape of an outcome, not to describe one.

Copy

  • The order: cash, law, people, reputation
  • A payment schedule whose second key is its sharpest sceptic
  • Creditors by class, reasons logged
  • An options appraisal whose legality line someone fills in
  • Saying only what is known

Do not copy

  • The numbers, thresholds or timetable: they belong to this charity’s size and funding mix
  • The base case: this board got its grant in week five, and the plan was built so that it would not have mattered if it had not
